The course has two paper seminars of 2 hours each. Each participant presents in only one of them, leading the discussion of one paper with a short summary and questions for the group. In the other seminar, participants take part in the discussion. Leading one discussion is part of the [examination](../07-examination/).

Each participant chooses one paper on one of the two seminar topics below, and with it the seminar in which they present. Participants send their choice to the examiner at the start of the course. The examiner confirms each choice, so that no paper is presented twice, and shares the list of chosen papers. Before each seminar, all participants read every paper that is discussed in it, not only their own, so that the group can discuss them.

## Paper seminar 1: generative model families {#paper-seminar-1-generative-model-families}

This seminar extends Lecture 2 (VAEs and GANs) and Lecture 3 (diffusion models, transformers and basics of LLMs). Participants choose a paper on one of these topics.

## Paper seminar 2: privacy and fairness {#paper-seminar-2-privacy-and-fairness}

This seminar extends Lecture 5 (privacy) and Lecture 6 (fairness). A paper fits the topic if it studies the privacy or the fairness of synthetic data, or both. For each paper, ask what it implies for the decision to release a synthetic dataset (ILO3).

## Leading a discussion

{{% steps %}}

### Read the paper closely

Read the paper twice: once for its argument, once for its assumptions, its evaluation and what it leaves out.

### Prepare a short summary

Summarise the problem the paper addresses, its method and its main results. Connect it to the lecture it extends.

### Prepare questions for the group

Write questions that open a discussion, for example on the paper's assumptions, its threat model or fairness definition, its evaluation, or what it means for the datasets participants work with.

### Lead the discussion

Present the summary, then lead the group through the questions.
